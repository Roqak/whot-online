# Product

## Register

product

The game table is the product. The landing page is treated as **brand** register per task: it is how new players arrive from shared links and social posts, so it needs real presence, not just a form.

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

- **Nigerians and the diaspora** who grew up on Whot. Mostly on Android phones, often budget devices on mobile data, sometimes outdoors in bright light. They play in short casual sessions, with friends through a shared link or against bots while waiting. They know the rules and want speed and banter.
- **Global casual players** arriving from web portals (CrazyGames) or a friend's link, many of whom have never heard of Whot. They need to understand what to do within the first turn, without reading rules.

The job: start a game in seconds and enjoy playing it, whether you are a veteran or meeting Whot for the first time.

## Product Purpose

Last Card brings Whot, the Nigerian card game, online: create a table, share a link, play friends or bots, no signup. It ships as a website (lastcard.fun), an Android app, an Android TV app and a portal build.

Success: a new player finishes their first round without confusion, and wants another. A returning player gets from the home screen to their first card in one or two taps.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, lively, Naija.** It should feel like a real game with your people: confident, a little loud, proudly Nigerian without leaning on clichés (no flag colours as decoration, no stock "African pattern" wallpaper). Copy talks like a friend at the table: short, direct, a bit of humour. Moments that matter (Last Card, Pick Two, checking up) get real energy; everything else stays out of the way.

Last Card has its own identity. Other Nigerian products (e.g. Lagos Life) are references for how things work, never for how things look.

## Anti-references

- **A Lagos Life knock-off.** No borrowing its palette, fonts, layout or branding.
- **Casino and betting apps.** No gold-on-black slot machine glamour, flashing coins or gambling feel. This is a family card game.
- **Generic AI-made app.** No decorative gradients, glassy cards, purple-blue SaaS styling or template layouts.
- **Kiddie cartoon games.** No over-bright, bouncy, toy-like styling.
- **Cheap ad-heavy mobile games.** No cluttered screens, popups everywhere or noisy competing buttons.

## Design Principles

1. **The table explains itself.** A newcomer should know whose turn it is, what can be played and why not, from the screen alone. Rules sheets are a backup, not the onboarding.
2. **Never punish a slip.** Irreversible moves need a deliberate second action (card play stays tap to lift, tap again or flick to play). Speed comes from clarity, not from removing confirmation.
3. **Energy where the game has it.** Special cards, last card and wins get sound, motion and colour; idle chrome stays quiet so those moments land.
4. **Built for the phone in your hand.** Thumb reach, one-handed play, cheap screens, sunlight and slow networks are the default conditions, not edge cases.
5. **Warm, not loud.** Personality through copy, colour and small touches, never through clutter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Readable outdoors:** strong contrast on budget screens in bright light; aim for WCAG AA as a floor for text and key UI.
- **Colour-blind safe:** never rely on colour alone for playable, your turn, danger or win states; always pair with shape, motion, position or text. Card shapes already carry meaning by form.
- **Reduced motion:** respect the OS setting (MotionConfig `reducedMotion="user"` is already in place); essential feedback must still read without animation.
- **Low-end phones:** effects stay cheap (transforms and opacity, no heavy blur or large shadows animating) so play is smooth on budget Androids.
